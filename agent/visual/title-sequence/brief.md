## 3. The idea

A summary piece in the form of a title sequence. We watch our avatar, **the Director**, being built from nothing: it starts on a single chip and ends on his finished face. Every shot is a micro close-up of one stage of the build. On-screen titles run through it like film credits and sell the studio. The final shot pulls back to reveal him complete, then the line and the logo.

The point it makes: the Director is assembled with intent, piece by piece. That is the difference between direction and generation.

## 4. The Director (character rules)

He is a futurist avatar of us as directors, built from my own likeness. He is not a generic AI robot.

- Glossy white visor shell over the forehead and eyes, with a centre seam and a perforated mesh band at eye level.
- Matte white cracked ceramic face, white beard, long white filament hair swept back.
- White chest armour with embossed relief in three scales: winding channels, concentric rings, map-like blocks, fine micro detail.
- Open shoulder joint with cable bundles; neck with tubes.
- **Finish: only the visor is glossy. Face, neck, hands and armour are always matte.**
- All white. No black parts. No glow.

## 5. Look

- **One style:** matte white ceramic and embossed relief, greyscale, with one gloss accent (the visor).
- **Two tones only:** light (white studio, soft high-key light, white on white) or dark (near-black room, one soft key light from the upper left, hair may fall into shadow). Pick one for the whole sequence, or move from dark to light as he completes. See section 9.
- **Colour values:** light page `#FFFFFF`, dark page `#0B0B0D`.
- **Never:** neon or cyberpunk, glowing or pulsing circuitry, chrome sci-fi robots, gradient blobs, AI sparkle, lens flares, particles floating in the air.

## 6. Structure (first draft, to be challenged)

Target 45 to 60 seconds, 16:9. Roughly ten shots of 4 to 6 seconds.

| # | Stage of the build | What we see, in macro |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | The chip | A single all-white chip on a matte white surface. First component seats into place. |
| 2 | Relief | Channels and rings being engraved into a white ceramic plate. |
| 3 | Frame | Spine segments and the waist ring locking together. |
| 4 | Cables | Cable bundles threading through the open shoulder joint. |
| 5 | Armour | A chest plate lowering onto the frame; relief catches the light. |
| 6 | Neck | Clear tubes seating into the neck collar. |
| 7 | Face | The ceramic face surface; hairline cracks, a port. |
| 8 | Hair and beard | White filaments, extreme close-up. |
| 9 | The visor | The glossy shell lowers and seats. The mesh band is the last thing in focus. First and only gloss in the film. |
| 10 | Reveal | Slow pull back to the three-quarter head and shoulders. Hold. Title, then logo. |

## 9. Decisions to make with me first

1. **Tone:** all dark, all light, or dark to light as he completes?
2. **Camera:** separate locked-off macro shots cut together, or one continuous pull-back from the chip to the reveal? Continuous is stronger but much harder to keep stable.
3. **Sound:** music only, sound design of the build, or a voiceover?
4. **Length:** 30, 45 or 60 seconds?
5. **Titles:** credit-style cards as drafted, or fewer and bigger?
6. **Service words:** the service is AI creative direction across film, animation, social and digital. Which output words appear on screen, if any?

## 10. Production notes

- **Tools:** Higgsfield. Stills with GPT Image 2 (best at holding the character) and Nano Banana Pro (most faithful on edits). Video with Seedance 2.5, generated at 720p by default.
- **Method:** make an approved still for the start (and where useful the end) of each shot first, then animate from the stills. Always attach the character sheets as references.
- **Prompt style I want:** plain production language. No flowery wording, no buzzwords, few adjectives. Structure every prompt as: 1) Subject, 2) Environment, 3) Action, 4) Camera, 5) Lighting, 6) Constraints.
- **Known failure modes to design against:** object mutation and morphing geometry, environmental drift, unwanted light pulsing, subject transformation mid-shot, gloss creeping onto matte surfaces, circuit patterns that start to glow, hands and joints deforming.
- **Lessons from the stills work:** naming something in a negative ("no abs") tends to produce it, so describe what you want positively. Micro detail needs 4k and close framing. Reference the approved image for exact carry-over instead of re-describing it.
